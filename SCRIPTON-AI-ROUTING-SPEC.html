Two things this table changes about your current setup

Output ceilings are no longer a constraint. The whole continuation-pass machinery in developSceneList() and planScenes() exists to work around an 8,192-token output cap. Every current model returns 64–128K. A 90-scene map now fits in one call — the continuation loop should stay as a safety net, but it should stop firing.

Your Gemini engine is pinned to a model id that does not resolve. gemini-3.5-flash is in your catalog and was the model on the failing Gemini rows in AiRun. The current Flash tier is gemini-3.6-flash.

The retirement horizon rule

No model near its retirement date may hold a default. Encode it as a computed guard rather than a hand-maintained list, so it stays true without anyone editing a table — you already learned what a stale hard-coded model id costs.

  • Inside 180 days → barred from any default. Not a profile assignment, not a primary rung.
  • Inside 90 days → barred entirely. Removed from ladders and hidden from the model dropdown; an engine row still pinned to one is migrated forward on boot, exactly as RETIRED_MODELS already does for models that have passed.
  • Surface it. Add retiresOn to the catalog entries and show a countdown chip in Engines & Routing at 180 days. Anthropic gives at least 60 days' notice; the guard should fire long before that.

Applied on 31 Aug 2026 this bars Haiku 4.5 (45 days) outright and drops Sonnet 4.6 (170 days) — your current live default — to fallback-only.

The consequence: Anthropic has no cheap tier left

Haiku 4.5 was the only sub-$1 Anthropic option, and the current lineup announces no successor. Every mechanical task that was going to run there — format validation, slugline consistency, name casing — now has two honest homes, and neither is another model call by default.

Most of it should be code. Slugline grammar, character-cue casing, page counts and revision-mark consistency are deterministic checks that a parser does perfectly, instantly and for nothing. Your scene-parse.util.ts already does most of the work. Route only genuine judgment calls to a model.

Where a model is genuinely needed, cross-vendor is now dramatically cheaper than anything Anthropic offers: gpt-5.6-luna at $0.20 / $1.20 is a fifth of Haiku's price with five times the context.

02 — Model-to-stage mapping

Five stages, five different jobs

These are numbered because they are a real sequence — each stage consumes the previous stage's output, and a fault at 02 propagates to everything downstream. The assignments below are capability-tier judgments grounded in documented model characteristics: price, latency, output ceiling, and whether extended thinking helps or hurts the task. Where a call is genuinely arguable I have said so, and every one of them is A/B-testable through the same settings surface.

The governing economics: stage 03 makes 55–90 model calls per feature. Stages 01, 02 and 05 make between one and six. Craft budget belongs where the call count is low; cost discipline belongs at stage 03.

Why Opus 5. This stage sets constraints every later stage inherits — the spine, the era and culture rules, the lore policy, the canon seed. An error here is not a bad paragraph, it is a bad script. It is also the stage where your knowledge/ packs push 30K+ tokens of era, genre and conflict material into context and expect the model to hold all of it coherently. Low call volume plus high blast radius plus heavy context is the exact shape that justifies the expensive model. 03 — Complete-length generation

Your pipeline is aiming at a short film

The stated goal is a 90–120 page feature running 80–110 minutes. Two independent settings in the generator make that arithmetically impossible, and they compound. This is the finding to act on before anything else in this document.

Error one — the scene target is roughly half a feature

generateFeatureAsync() sets the scene count as Math.min(90, Math.max(55, beatN * 1.5)), defaulting to 60 scenes, and planScenes() hard-caps its return at scenes.slice(0, 100).

Across 12,000+ produced screenplays the average feature contains 110 scenes — just over one scene per page against a median of 106 pages. Action averages 131. Your ceiling of 90 sits below the average feature, and your default of 60 sits at 55% of it. Even a flawless run produces a script that is structurally a novella.

Error two — "one page" is specified as a fifth of a page

The writeScene system prompt says the scene "MUST fit on ONE page — roughly 8 to 16 short lines of action and dialogue COMBINED". Those two instructions contradict each other. A standard screenplay page is 55 lines of 12pt Courier — which is exactly what your own paginate() uses (const per = 55). Eight to sixteen lines is a fifth of a page, not a page.

Compounded: 60 scenes × ~15 lines including the heading and blanks ≈ 900 lines ≈ 16 pages. That is the real output ceiling of the current configuration, and it explains a "complete" script that files as DONE and reads as an outline.

The fix: budget pages, not scenes

Scene count should be derived from a page target, and each planned scene should carry an explicit page allocation that the writer is told to hit. Real features do not consist of uniform one-page scenes — they run from quarter-page cutaways to three-page set pieces — so a flat per-scene length is wrong even at the right count.

The page-per-minute rule is a planning heuristic, not a law — across 2,520 scripts the mean ratio is 1.1 pages per minute (a page runs about 55 seconds), and only 18% of scripts land within ±5% of it. It is precise enough to size a generation run and not precise enough to promise a runtime, which is exactly how the UI should present it: "≈ 100 min", never "100 min".

Six changes to scripton.service.ts

  • Add targetPages to the brief (default 105, band 90–120), and derive targetMinutes = targetPages / genreRatio for display.
  • Derive the scene count from pages: targetScenes = round(targetPages × sceneDensity(genre)) — 1.04 baseline, 1.24 action, 0.93 comedy. Replace the beatN * 1.5 heuristic, which measures the outline rather than the film.
  • Give every planned scene a pageWeight ∈ {0.25, 0.5, 1, 1.5, 2, 3}, assigned by the planner from dramatic function, constrained so Σ pageWeight ≈ targetPages. Add it to the planScenes JSON contract.
  • Pass a line budget to writeScene: lines = round(pageWeight × 55). Replace "roughly 8 to 16 short lines" with the computed figure and a ±20% tolerance. This one edit is the difference between 16 pages and 105.
  • Raise the plan cap from slice(0, 100) to slice(0, ceil(targetScenes × 1.3)) — the current cap truncates any action feature.
  • Close the loop. You already verify narrative completeness with verifyEnding(). Add verifyLength(): if the finished draft is under 90% of targetPages, do not file it as DONE — run an expansion pass over the scenes that came in furthest under their allocation. A run that reaches the final beat but lands at 70 pages is not complete.

Why this matters more than model choice

No model upgrade fixes a length target that is set too low. Opus 5 asked for 60 quarter-page scenes will produce 60 excellent quarter-page scenes and file a 16-page document. Fix the budget first; the routing in the rest of this document then determines how good those 110 scenes are, not how many of them there are.

04 — Implementation

You already built this. One function is missing.

The temptation with a brief like this is to specify a new subsystem. That would be the wrong call: LlmRoutingPolicy already persists a capability, a fallbackChain, an allowedEngineIds set and two override flags, per ORG and per PROJECT. The Engines & Routing UI already renders five capability tabs with reorderable failover. resolveChain() already honours all of it.

The entire per-stage routing feature is blocked on one function that hardcodes its answer:

// llm-routing.service.ts — today
capabilityForTask(_task?: string): string { return 'LLM_DEFAULT'; }

Replace it with a real mapping and every stage above becomes independently routable, configurable and failover-aware — with no new tables, no new endpoints and no change to AiService:

/**
 * Map a dotted task string (surface.purpose.stage) onto its routing capability.
 * The five capabilities are the five writing stages: each has its own engine,
 * its own model and its own failover ladder, set in Engines & Routing.
 * Longest-prefix wins, so 'scripton.develop.scenes' beats 'scripton.develop'.
 */
const TASK_CAPABILITY: Array<[string, string]> = [
  ['scripton.research',          'IDEATION'],
  ['scripton.adapt',             'IDEATION'],
  ['scripton.lore',              'IDEATION'],
  ['scripton.develop',           'STRUCTURE'],
  ['scripton.feature.plan',      'STRUCTURE'],
  ['scripton.feature.scene',     'SCENE'],
  ['scripton.vertical',          'SCENE'],
  ['scripton.doc',               'SCENE'],
  ['scripton.dialogue',          'DIALOGUE'],
  ['scripton.dialect',           'DIALOGUE'],
  ['scripton.coverage',          'DOCTOR'],
  ['scripton.diagnostics',       'DOCTOR'],
  ['scripton.rating',            'DOCTOR'],
  ['scripton.compare',           'DOCTOR'],
  ['scripton.feature.coverage',  'DOCTOR'],
  ['scripton.canon',             'DOCTOR'],
  ['scripton.culture',           'DOCTOR'],
];

capabilityForTask(task?: string): string {
  const t = String(task || '');
  let best = 'LLM_DEFAULT', bestLen = 0;
  for (const [prefix, cap] of TASK_CAPABILITY) {
    if (t === prefix || t.startsWith(prefix + '.')) {
      if (prefix.length > bestLen) { best = cap; bestLen = prefix.length; }
    }
  }
  return best;
}

Three supporting changes

1. Widen the capability list. CAPABILITIES is currently ['LLM_DEFAULT','BREAKDOWN','DRAFTING','POLISH','LEGAL'] — invented before the stages were understood. Replace with ['LLM_DEFAULT','IDEATION','STRUCTURE','SCENE','DIALOGUE','DOCTOR','LEGAL']. getRouting() already synthesises an empty policy for any capability with no row, so this is additive and needs no migration.

2. Route raw() too. Vision and forced-tool calls bypass the chain entirely and pin to Anthropic. That is defensible, but they should still read their model from the capability policy rather than from an env var.

3. Add per-capability generation params. temperature and maxTokens belong on the policy, not hard-coded at each call site. Scene writing wants 0.85; coverage wants 0.3. Today both inherit whatever the caller passed.

What each profile actually trades

Economy drops the separate dialogue pass and runs doctoring on Sonnet 5 rather than Opus 5. Structure holds — Sonnet 5 is genuinely capable of a coherent 114-scene map — but subtext gets thinner and coverage notes get blunter. Right for exploratory drafts and for the fourth regeneration of the same act.

Balanced puts Opus 5 everywhere the call count is low and Sonnet 5 where it is high. This is the profile that should ship as default.

Master moves scene construction to Opus 5 as well. Honest assessment: this is the weakest-value step on the list — it triples the cost of the highest-volume stage for a quality gain that a separate Opus 5 dialogue pass already delivers more cheaply. Offer it, default away from it.

Best Script is the cross-vendor profile — the strongest model from each of the three houses at the stage where its character helps most, with the weak options removed entirely. See below; it is not simply "the expensive one".

Note what is not in the economy column: cross-vendor models. Gemini 3.6 Flash is cheaper than Sonnet 5, but mixing vendors inside a screenplay's prose stages costs voice consistency — the thing section 07 exists to protect. Cheaper models belong on the failover ladder, not in a default profile.

Best Script — mixing vendors without breaking the voice

The instinct behind "best of each vendor" is sound, but applied naively it destroys the thing that matters most: a screenplay written by three different models does not read like one writer. The resolution is that the five stages are not equally voice-bearing.

Structure, research and coverage produce analysis — JSON, notes, a beat map. Nobody reads their prose. Scene and dialogue produce the script. So Best Script mixes freely on the analytical stages and stays on a single pinned vendor for the two that reach the page.

One overlay that is not a genre

Script language. Your actual slate runs Arabic as often as English, and register control — dialect, era-appropriate diction, the accents and eras knowledge packs — is the first thing to degrade when a model tiers down. For any non-English script the dialogue stage should hold at the top tier in every profile, Economy included, and the permissive-fallback rungs should be checked for real capability in that language before being armed. A cheaper model that writes fluent-but-wrong Arabic register is worse than a refusal, because nothing flags it.

Self-review is quarantined, not just labelled

If coverage falls back in-house — the model that wrote the script grading the script — the notes are still generated, because they have some value, but they are marked SELF_REVIEW and excluded from anything that carries weight: no coverage score, no greenlight input, no "notes addressed" count, and a visible banner on the panel saying an outside read is still pending.

The alternative — presenting self-review as ordinary coverage — is worse than having no coverage at all, because a writer acts on it believing it came from an outside eye. A model is blind to its own habits by construction; a clean score from the author model is not evidence, and the UI should not let it read as evidence.

Policy field: "selfReview": "QUARANTINE" — alternatives are "BLOCK" (produce nothing until an outside engine is available) and "ALLOW" (label only). Default QUARANTINE.

The real cost lever is not the model

Scene generation is input-dominated: each of 110 calls re-sends the same feature context — the brief, the language directive, the knowledge pack, the spine — around 5,000 tokens, unchanged, 110 times. That is roughly 550K input tokens per feature spent re-reading identical text.

Anthropic prompt caching charges cache reads at 0.1× base input and a 1-hour cache write at 2×. Sonnet 5's minimum cacheable prefix is 1,024 tokens; your context block is about five times that. Placing one cache_control breakpoint after the shared context collapses 550K billable input tokens to roughly 65K.

Saving: about 60% of the scene stage, on every profile, with no quality cost whatsoever. It requires one change in providers.ts — splitting the Anthropic user payload into content blocks so a breakpoint can be placed — because callProvider currently sends a single flat string.

Schema addition

The profile is one field on the settings object, resolved before the capability policies are read. It selects a named preset; the policies remain the source of truth, so an override at any stage simply wins.

07 — Resilience

Failover that understands screenwriting

Generic failover treats every failure as "try the next one". That is wrong here in two specific ways, and both are cheap to fix.

A refusal is not an outage

When a model declines a scene on content grounds, retrying the identical prompt across four more providers spends four more calls to collect four more refusals — and then reports "no provider could complete the request", which tells the writer nothing about what actually happened. Refusal needs its own error class alongside the CREDIT / RATE / AUTH / MODEL kinds already in providers.ts, and the default policy should be SURFACE: stop the chain, tell the writer which scene was declined and why, and offer a single explicit retry on a more permissive engine.

Voice consistency outranks availability

If Anthropic rate-limits at scene 47 of 90 and the chain silently fails over, scenes 47–52 are written by a different model with a different prose instinct — inside one screenplay, under one revision colour. Nobody will know why act three reads differently. For the SCENE and DIALOGUE stages, set pinPerBuild: true: the first successful engine is recorded on the build version and every subsequent scene uses it. If it becomes unavailable, pause the run rather than switching mid-script, and record any forced switch on the revision so the UI can say "scenes 47–52 drafted on a fallback engine".

Tiered ladders per stage

Tier 1 is always in-family — same vendor, adjacent model — because it preserves prose character. Cross-vendor is tier 2, and only reached when Anthropic as a whole is unavailable.

Build order

1. Fix the length budget first (§03). Nothing else on this list matters while the generator targets 60 quarter-page scenes. The line-budget change to writeScene and the page-derived scene count are the difference between a 16-page document and a feature — and they are independent of every routing decision here.
2. Ship the horizon guard and repoint the stale engine rows — Anthropic to claude-sonnet-5, Gemini to gemini-3.6-flash, Local to a model Ollama has actually pulled. This gives you a working failover chain for the first time; on 27 August you had none.
3. Turn on prompt caching (§06). One cache_control breakpoint after the feature context. Roughly 48% off every run, at zero quality cost — and the saving grows now that scene count is going up.
4. Ship capabilityForTask() and the widened capability list. Per-stage routing goes live with no UI work.
5. Add params and profiles to the policy; remove the hard-coded temperatures from the call sites.
6. Add the REFUSAL class and pinPerBuild. Voice consistency over availability, per §07.
