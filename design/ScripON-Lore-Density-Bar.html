WHY IT WORKS — and the one catch the research flagged

The note IS the instruction

The text you read at each step is the same text injected into the AI brief — not UX flavour. “What you read is what the model gets,” so the control is legible: you always know what you just told it.

Stepped, not a raw number

Research on steering models is clear: a bare “density = 0.62” is followed unreliably. Named levels with a definition + concrete anchors (which act-beats, what share of scenes) steer far more consistently. So the bar snaps to 6 defined stops.

Two readouts, one truth

You see the friendly note; the model also gets structural anchors (premise/midpoint/climax flags, scene-share %). Same decision, expressed for a human and for the machine — that pairing is the novel bit.

DON'T CONFUSE IT WITH PER-ELEMENT WEIGHT
Lore density — the global dial

How far the whole layer bends the grounded story, from Accent → Saturated. One per build.

Element role + weight — per element

Which single element owns which beats — djinn as antagonist (spine), ghoul as obstacle (accent). Many per build.

Together: the density bar sets the overall pressure; each element's role/weight says where that pressure lands. Both ride into the brief — on top of your Work source, which still does the storytelling.
