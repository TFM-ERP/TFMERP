Logline
A burned-out fixer has one night to move a witness across a city that wants them both dead — before the man hunting them turns out to be the only one she trusts.
Synopsis
Sarah Chen runs errands no one admits to. A routine pickup becomes a manhunt across a nocturnal city of brokers and bad debts. The first act is taut and the dialogue crackles; momentum stalls at the midpoint — a long stretch of Sarah-only scenes — before a strong, well-earned third act where the man hunting her is revealed as the one person she trusted. The Broker's motive is asserted but never dramatised, and this read does not smooth that over.

03 — COMMENTS
Comments
Specific, honest analysis — every concern cites a scene; the read names real weaknesses rather than flattering the draft.

PLOT

A propulsive one-night clock drives Acts 1 and 3. The midpoint sags — pp 51–63 runs eleven consecutive Sarah-only scenes — and the inciting reversal lands a touch late. Resolution is earned and satisfying.

CHARACTERS

Sarah is a fully realised lead with a genuine arc (Sc 1 vs Sc 64). Riley is a principled foil. The antagonist's motive is asserted at Sc 47 but never dramatised.

DIALOGUE

Distinct and propulsive, rich in subtext across 38 speaking scenes; a clear standout. A few Act 2 exchanges turn on-the-nose and could carry more silence.

THEME

Trust versus survival — what loyalty costs when everyone is for sale. Resonant and woven through action rather than stated.

ORIGINALITY

A familiar chase frame elevated by a fixer's POV and a singular voice. Not a new premise, but a bankable, well-executed lane.

MARKETABILITY

Clear genre lane with festival and platform potential. Budget-tier comps performed; audience skews male 25+ with crossover on the strength of the lead.

PRODUCTION

64 scenes, night-heavy (a real consolidation opportunity), ~12 locations. Feasible at a mid tier; trimming the Act 2 sag also reduces shoot days. Grounded in the real breakdown and schedule.
