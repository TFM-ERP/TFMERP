Arabian · desert folklore
Ghoul · الغول
folklore · openhorrorjinn-kin
ORIGIN

A shapeshifting desert demon of pre-Islamic Arabian lore, counted among the jinn-kin. Lures lone travellers off the road to devour them; also robs graves and preys on the dead.

REGIONAL VARIANTS

Si’lat — female form that passes as human · Qutrub — restless frenzied ghoul · Persian & Turkic cognates across the trade routes.

STORY HOOKS
▸The hired guide who knows the dunes too well — and never eats.
▸A grave-robbing that wakes something older than the tomb.
▸The traveller who realises the voice calling their name is wearing a dead friend’s face.
SACRED / CARE

Folklore creature — open to use. Jinn-kin tag noted; the guardrail keeps it as lore, never tied to scripture or named deity.

FRAME 4 — SACRED-POLICY TIERS · living-religion locked, historical pantheons allowed-with-flag, folklore open
Sacred policy — what the Atlas will and won’t surface
Living-religion sacred — God, prophets, living-faith deities
Never depicted, named, or voiced. Not listed in the Atlas at all.
e.g. Abrahamic prophets & God · Hindu deities · Orisha · Buddhist figures
HARD-LOCKED
Historical / classical pantheons
Deities of religions no longer practised — selectable, but flagged for review. Toggle the policy per project.
e.g. Greek · Norse · Egyptian · Mesopotamian gods & heroes
ALLOWED · FLAGGED
Folklore beings & creatures
Djinn, ghoul, yokai, draugr, rusalka, aswang… open to use, each with its own tag.
all 12 cultures · the bulk of the library
OPEN
The tier sets the default; the per-project toggle on the middle tier lets a Greek-myth or Norse-saga production switch on classical pantheons while the hard lock on living-faith sacred figures never moves. Whatever you choose is written into the brief’s guardrail.
