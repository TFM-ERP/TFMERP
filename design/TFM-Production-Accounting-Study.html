🎬 Production-Accounting Industry Study & Gap Analysis

TFM-System · Validation Study

Is the budgeting & cost engine ready for real productions?

A deep look at how professional film/TV productions run budgets, commitments, payroll and actuals from script breakdown to final cost report — benchmarked against the leading platforms and measured against what TFM has built today, with a phased plan to close the gaps before launch.

Verdict. The TFM cost engine is already well above indie-tool level and close to studio-grade in several areas most systems never reach: a true locked-budget lifecycle (Draft→Review→Approved→Locked→Working) with an audit log, a real fringe/labor-burden engine, a Budget→Committed→Actual→ETC→EFC→Variance cost report, PO approval + invoicing, petty cash, AP with three-way-match and segregation-of-duties, a shared period-close lock, and contract/deal-memo→auto-PO encumbrance. The work that remains before it can be trusted by studios, line producers, accountants and financiers is concentrated in five places: (1) the project ledger and the double-entry GL are not reconciled, (2) forecasting isn't schedule-driven, (3) commitments bind to a cost-center code rather than a budget line, (4) several real-world transaction types are missing (purchase requests, cash advances, card feeds, project expense claims), and (5) there is no Daily Production Report / financier reporting pack. None of these are architectural dead-ends — they are additive, and the spine to hang them on already exists.

1The industry-standard workflow

Every professional production runs the same financial spine. Creative decisions enter as a script breakdown, become a schedule, drive a budget, and that budget is locked as a baseline before principal photography. From that point the budget is never edited — it is tracked against, and changed only by formal revision.

Script → Breakdown (elements) → Schedule (stripboard) → Budget → LOCK ▣ → Commitments (PR→PO, deal memos) → Actuals (AP, petty cash, payroll, cards) → Forecast (EFC) → General Ledger → Cost Report / Final Cost

1.1 Locked budget vs actuals — the core discipline

Productions do not keep a single editable budget. They keep the approved/locked budget as a fixed baseline and track everything against it. The living document is the Weekly Cost Report (WCR), the line-producer/accountant's instrument that, per cost-center, shows:

ColumnMeaning
BudgetThe locked baseline (only moved by approved revisions/transfers/overages → the "revised budget").
Cost this week / to dateActuals paid or incurred (cumulative).
CommittedOpen purchase-order value not yet invoiced (encumbrance).
ETC (Estimate to Complete)The accountant's forward estimate of what's left to spend on the line.
EFC (Estimated Final Cost)= Actual + Committed + ETC. The number everyone watches.
Variance= Budget − EFC. Drives "find the money or cut" decisions; the bond company watches this weekly.

Revisions are handled by creating a new budget version (or by approved transfers between lines and approved overages that lift a line) — never by silently editing the locked baseline. Most productions therefore hold several versions: Draft Approved Locked Revised (V2, V3…) and a continuously-updated Forecast/EFC view.

1.2 Hiring cast & crew → cost

A negotiated deal memo fixes the rate; that value becomes a commitment against a budget line (an encumbrance). Actual employment cost then accrues through timecards (days, overtime, turnaround/meal penalties) plus per diems, travel, accommodation, box/kit rentals, and is reconciled against the budgeted amount as a per-person/per-line variance. The deal-memo value, the commitment and the actuals must all reference the same budget line for the variance to be meaningful.

1.3 Fringes & labor burden

Labor never costs its face rate. Productions add fringes — automatically calculated percentages/flat amounts on gross wages: payroll taxes (FICA/SS, Medicare, FUTA/SUI), workers' comp, union pension & health (SAG-AFTRA ≈ 21%, DGA ≈ 8.75% pension + 11.25% health, IATSE flat-per-hour + %), guild dues, plus a payroll-service handling fee. Total burden commonly runs 22–34% on top of wages. Per diems are non-taxable reimbursements; box/kit rentals are 1099/non-fringed. These belong in the budget from day one and must flow through to actuals and the forecast automatically.

1.4 Production-accounting transaction types & their cost-report effect

Purchase Request (requisition)Pre-commitment / soft encumbrance; approval converts it to a PO.
Purchase OrderAdds to Committed; reduces remaining; not yet an actual.

Underneath, everything is double-checked against the general ledger: the production books (cost report) and the company GL/trial balance must reconcile, and the final cost report at wrap is audited and tied to the GL before it goes to the studio/financier/bond.

4Recommendations — a phased, additive plan

All changes are additive and reversible; nothing requires tearing down the strong core. Ordered by what protects auditability and trust first.

PHASE 1 · before launch — integrity & audit
① GL posting bridge — post every project COST/INCOME actual to the double-entry journal so the cost report reconciles to the trial balance (this is the single biggest credibility item for accountants/financiers). ② PO → budget line — line-level commitments & variance. ③ Fold location/parallel rollups into cost-center actuals so the cost report is the one source of truth.
PHASE 2 · controls — procurement & cash
④ Purchase Requests (requisition→approve→PO). ⑤ Cash advances + float clearing. ⑥ Credit-card feed + coding + statement rec. ⑦ Project expense claims → reimbursement. ⑧ Per-project bank reconciliation.
PHASE 3 · forecasting & reporting
⑨ First-class Forecast — schedule/burn-driven ETC + scenario versions + variance alerts. ⑩ Daily Production Report + hot costs. ⑪ Financier/bond reporting pack (WCR + DPR + cashflow + cast/crew cost). ⑫ Batch production PayrollRun register.
PHASE 4 · sync integrity
⑬ Hard FKs across script→scene→strip→element→budget line→commitment→actual. ⑭ Change-propagation — breakdown/schedule edits raise budget & forecast warnings (the "creative decision changed a cost" alert).

Essential for enterprise/studio use (the must-haves)

If the goal is for studios, line producers, accountants and financiers to rely on TFM: the GL reconciliation (①), line-level commitments (②), purchase requests (④), a real forecast (⑨), and the DPR + reporting pack (⑩⑪) are the non-negotiables. Everything else is competitive polish.

5Proposed enterprise data model

The spine already largely exists in TFM. Solid arrows are links that exist today; dashed red are the missing/weak links from §3 that close the loop to true industry standard.

Relationship spine in words: Script.scene → Breakdown.element → Strip(schedule) → BudgetLineItem (the locked baseline) → committed by PurchaseRequest→PurchaseOrder.line and Contract/DealMemo → realized by ProjectTransaction (AP invoice / petty cash / card / cash-advance / expense-claim) and Timecard→PayrollRun (with the fringe engine) → continuously projected by Forecast (ETC/EFC) → posted to the General Ledger → reported in the Cost Report / WCR, frozen as Snapshots and reported daily as the DPR. Every node except PurchaseRequest, the GL bridge, schedule-driven Forecast and the DPR already exists; the redesign work is to make the commitment/actual links bind at the line level and to reconcile the project ledger to the GL.
