1 · The bar, and the gap we exploit

To beat coverage you must know what elite coverage is — and where every AI tool currently fails. Both are well documented.

What elite coverage actually is

Fixed anatomy: cover sheet (logline + one-line verdict) → 1–2pp synopsis → 2–3pp comments (the part that matters) → ratings. Studios grade a standard element set (premise, story, structure, character, dialogue, pacing, originality, marketability) and render PASS / CONSIDER / RECOMMEND on the script and the writer separately.HIGH

Base rates that frame the value: ~83% PASS, ~2–4% CONSIDER, RECOMMEND <5% (a reader may give 2–4 a year). A RECOMMEND is an alarm bell — staked on the reader's credibility. Human coverage runs $89–$500 over 7–14 days.

The reliability benchmark (Black List, 128k evals)

6 dimensions on 1–10; crucially overall is NOT an average — it's a holistic viability call. Two readers land within 1 point 73.8% of the time (≈ academic peer-review consistency).HIGH

Most-agreed dimensions: premise, setting (bounded). Least-agreed: dialogue, plot, character — "taste, not craft." Design implication: let AI score the bounded dimensions confidently; treat dialogue/character/plot conservatively and keep a human in the loop. (One unrebutted critique: the 73.8% is partly score-compression — ~83% of scores sit 5–7. Cite both.)

Where AI coverage fails today — the 2025 evidence our opening

The Motion Picture Editors Guild ran ~100 union story analysts against six AI platforms (Variety, Oct 2025): loglines — AI matched humans; synopses — "11th-grade-essay" weak; complex scripts — AI misattributes action and hallucinates plot points; notes/analysis — "an almost total fail"; and the killer, sycophancy — models "weren't evaluating, they were cheerleading… it wants to please you." WB analyst: "The most important thing I'm looking for is 'Do I care?' An LLM can't care."HIGH

Tellingly, the leading AI-coverage tool (ScriptSense, now Cinelytic) deliberately withholds the PASS/RECOMMEND verdict — its founder: AI "will make something up… we're not there yet." Greenlight Coverage gave a 20-years-unsold script a "recommend." Largo/Cinelytic concede misses (Largo/Adú 75%, wrong range). ScriptBook pivoted; StoryFit (raised $11M) closed; Coverfly, WeScreenplay, ScreenCraft coverage all shut in 2025. AI coverage now runs $10–45 in minutes — that's the market we enter, and accuracy/trust is the unmet need.

Lexicon sentiment misreads sarcasm/genre diction — smooth heavily, validate against TRIPOD structure. 4 · Coverage rubric v2 — your house format, exceeded

Keep the established house anatomy (DATA sheet + COVERAGE header → synopsis → comments×7 [plot, characters, dialogue, theme, originality, marketability, production] → 5 comps → character breakdown with Scenes-%). Layer on, from the research:

  • 6-dimension 1–10 scores with an explicit note that overall ≠ the average (it's a viability judgement); show per-dimension confidence.
  • PASS / CONSIDER / RECOMMEND on script AND writer separately ("consider the writer, pass the script" is a real, useful outcome).
  • Specificity rule — every note cites the exact scene/page and the mechanism (the #1 divider of elite vs box-ticking coverage). Our scene links make this native.
  • Anti-sycophancy guardrail — the model must surface real problems, not cheerlead; coverage that only praises is flagged as incomplete.
  • "Don't fill the holes" — the synopsis must not paper over gaps the script has; a clean synopsis from a muddled script falsely flatters it.
  • Conservative on taste dimensions — dialogue/character/plot scored with humility + HITL; premise/setting/structure scored with confidence (grounded by the computed engine in §3).

5 · Character & look boards no performers

Per your direction, ScripON does not suggest real performers — no names, no photos. That removes the entire right-of-publicity / likeness surface. Casting support is reframed as character/role type guidance plus world / location / era look boards (no faces).

Role & character type profiles text, grounded

Per role: importance (lead/supporting), age range, physicality, energy, arc, and wardrobe direction — written from the script and grounded in our character data and computed Scenes-%. A casting director's brief, not a name.HIGH

No real or suggested performer is named; no headshots. The profile describes the character, so there is no likeness or publicity-rights exposure.

World / location / era look boards tone, not faces

Reference imagery for the script's settings and tone — locations, era, palette, texture — tied to the intake's place/era/culture fields and assembled into the lookbook. No faces, no people-as-casting.HIGH

The only rights surface left is ordinary image licensing for these boards — handled in §6.

Why this is the safer and simpler design net win

Dropping real performers eliminates the hardest legal section of the original plan — right of publicity (CA §3344 / NY §50-51), Lanham false-endorsement, UK passing-off and GDPR on stored faces all fall away. What remains is genuinely useful to a producer (a grounded character/casting brief + a tonal lookbook) with none of the likeness risk, and no counsel gate on this feature.HIGH

7 · Market & comps intelligence — honest by design

Picking comps the way the industry does

Match on theme/story-style, budget band, target audience, recency (~last 5 years) — not plot similarity (the single most-cited practitioner mistake). Use a basket, not one title (5 minimum, 10 preferred, 20 for statistics). Budget-band rule of thumb: a $5M film draws comps ~$3–10M. Comps are the evidentiary basis financiers use to forecast revenue and value minimum guarantees — and ScripON can match them to the project's real budget tier.HIGH

Forecast honesty — the non-negotiable stance no guarantees

Ship ranges with confidence bands (P10/P50/P90) and a plain disclaimer — never a per-film "accuracy" promise. The evidence demands it: even vendors concede misses (Largo's own Adú test, 75%, wrong range); Cinelytic's "99%" is whole-market forecasting, not per-script; studios use these as "one signal" that "hasn't ever changed my mind"; academics call the models "far from satisfactory"; and the industry's own maxim — "nobody knows anything" — still holds. Honesty is a differentiator against tools that oversell.HIGH

8 · Scene-linked, living coverage interactive + revision-aware

Anchor to scenes, not text offsets

Every strength / concern / note links to a durable scene ID (scene number / sluglineID) so it survives rewrites — Google's own guidance warns text-offset anchors break between revisions. Click a note → jump to the exact scene in the Reader. Threads are resolvable and categorised (structure / dialogue / character / market…).HIGH

Coverage that updates with the draft

Reuse the WGA revision-colour sequence (White→Blue→Pink→…) + changed-pages + asterisk marks we already model, plus a scene-by-scene diff (à la Final Draft ScriptCompare / Scriptation) so coverage can say: "these 6 scenes changed since last coverage — here's how the read updates." Toggle a "what-changed" view vs a clean current-state view.HIGH

This is the feature no standalone coverage tool offers: a living report bound to the script's revision history via our existing colour-revision system + ScriptSyncLog — not a static PDF that's stale the moment the writer opens the file.

9 · Pitch-ready lookbook PDF financier-grade download

Anatomy financiers expect

A combined deck or a standalone lookbook (split it out when visuals dominate). Financier spine: logline + "for fans of" → snapshot → synopsis (reveal the ending) → characters (want/flaw/change) → tone & themes → visual rules + moodboard → audience snapshot → quantified comps (title/year/budget/outcome) → team → production plan → budget top-sheet (secured vs needed) → why-now → contact.HIGH

Lookbooks/mood reels are now "an accepted and usually required part of the process," sent to talent reps, financiers and sales agents at development. The frequent failure: "the money feels messy" — so the budget top-sheet must read clean.

How to generate it (tech)

Author the lookbook in HTML/CSS (our strength — same theme tokens) and render with Playwright (headless Chromium) + Paged.js for running headers / page numbers / clean pagination; charts as inline SVG, photos as CSS.HIGH

Design tokens: body 18–24pt, 2 fonts, restrained palette, embed fonts, target 5–15 MB, dated filename. Avoid React-PDF (no CSS Grid, weak absolute positioning), WeasyPrint (no JS charts), and wkhtmltopdf (dead since 2023). If print-shop typography is ever needed and there's budget: DocRaptor/Prince. Run a warm browser pool (≈150 MB/browser, ~5–10 pages each). We already have an app/print/coverage route — upgrade it into this engine.
