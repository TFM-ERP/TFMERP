TVC as a First-Class Production — and the Brief→Script Compass

Television commercials share prep/shoot/wrap with features but are a different organism: brief-driven, deliverable-centric, agency-governed, and compressed into 1–3 shoot days. This study covers how AI can read the brief and project it forward into the script, and exactly what should change when a producer picks TVC at project creation.

1 A TVC is a different organism than a feature

They look similar on the call sheet, but the spine is different. A feature is scene/page-driven; a TVC is brief- and deliverable-driven.

Feature / long-formTVC / commercial
Origin documentScreenplay (the script is the start)Creative brief → director's treatment → board/script. The brief is the "North Star."
Who owns itProducer / studioBrand → agency → production company (client-vendor). The agency holds the brief; the production house bids (AICP form).
Unit of workScene → strip → shoot day (pages/eighths)Deliverable / spot version — one shoot yields 15–30 deliverables.
ScheduleWeeks–months; DOOD by page count1–3 shoot days; prep-heavy. DOOD-by-page is largely irrelevant.
The "output"One locked cut / reelsCutdowns (60/30/15/6s) × aspect ratios (16:9, 9:16, 1:1) × captions/platform exports.
BudgetMovie-Magic chart; ETC/EFC over a long tailAICP bid (lettered sections A–X), client-vendor comparison, tight prep-loaded lines.
TalentCast contracts / SAG scaleUsage / buyout: territory, media, window; SAG-AFTRA holding fees every 13 weeks; exclusivity.
Sign-off gateGreenlight / locked budgetPPM (Pre-Production Meeting) — agency + client approve boards, wardrobe, locations, cast before the shoot.
The producer's real question on a TVC is not "what page are we on" — it's "are we still on brief, and can this footage cut into every deliverable the client bought." That is what the system should help answer.

2 What the system does today

The good news

ProductionProject.projectType already exists and is pickable at creation (TVC is the default): TVC · CORPORATE · DOCUMENTARY · FEATURE · SHORT · MUSIC_VIDEO · OTHER. It drives the projects-grid label/poster and one labor-cap rule.

The gap

Nothing in the workflow changes when you pick TVC. There is no brief and no treatment level; no deliverables/versions (cutdowns/aspect ratios); no usage/buyout tracking; no PPM gate. A commercial is run through the identical feature machinery — script hub, page/scene stripboard, DOOD-by-page, the long-form budget chart.

Net: projectType is a stored label, not a behaviour. The producer who picks "TVC" still gets a feature's tools — and gets no help connecting the client's brief to what actually has to be shot and delivered.

3 The Brief → Treatment → Script compass (your ask #1)

In commercials the chain is Brief → Treatment → Board/Script → Deliverables. The brief is the start and the judge. AI's highest-value role is to read the brief, carry it forward, and keep the producer aware of drift at each level.

A persistent on-brief scorecard sits across ②→⑤: "is the script still delivering what the brief bought?"

① Brief intake

The brief becomes a first-class artifact at the top of the (renamed) Creative hub — uploaded, pasted, or built in-app. It's the level before the script that the system is missing today.

② AI structures it

AI reads the brief and extracts a structured creative brief: objective, single-minded proposition / key message, audience, tone, mandatories (logo, pack shot, legal supers, tagline, claims), required durations (60/30/15/6), aspect ratios, channels, budget tier, references. Stored, editable — the brief stops being a PDF nobody re-reads.

③ AI scaffolds forward

From the structured brief, AI drafts a treatment outline + script/board beats (reusing the existing breakdown AI). Not the final word — a fast, on-brief starting point the producer/director shapes. "Brief analysis → production-ready script in a morning."

The compass — "where am I heading": an on-brief alignment scorecard continuously checks the live treatment / script / board / deliverables against the brief's mandatories, key message, and required durations — exactly the projection/change-list pattern already built for script→strips. It answers: Key message present? Pack shot scripted? Legal super included? A 6-second cutdown viable from these beats? All bought aspect ratios coverable? Green where on-brief, amber where drifting.

Picking TVC should switch the project into a workflow profile — same engine underneath, a different cockpit. Here's what flips.

The TVC-defining addition: a Deliverables & Versions module

One shoot → many finished assets. This is where a TVC producer actually lives. A Deliverable = a finished spot at a length × ratio, each with its own post pipeline and delivery spec.

Master spotCutdownsAspect ratiosPer-asset pipeline
"Hero — 60s"60 · 30 · 15 · 616:9 · 9:16 · 1:1Offline → Online → Grade → Mix → Delivered (status + due date + spec per cell)

The matrix doubles as a QC checklist (captions, legal supers, codec/bitrate per platform) and feeds delivery deadlines into the schedule and the cost report.

Usage & buyout

Per spot/talent: territory, media, licence window, and a holding-fee clock (SAG-AFTRA: every 13 weeks = session fee) with renewal alerts; music licence cost + term. Ties into budget + a "rights expiring" warning.

PPM gate

A pre-production-meeting checklist (boards locked, cast approved, wardrobe, locations, schedule, legal) that agency + client sign off — the commercial equivalent of the budget-lock greenlight.

Agency / client

First-class agency + brand/client stakeholders on the project (beyond a single clientId), so approvals, the brief, and the bid all attribute correctly.

6 Phased, additive plan

P0
Brief level (no behaviour change elsewhere). Add BRIEF/TREATMENT doc kinds + the CreativeBrief model; brief intake (upload/paste) in the Creative hub; AI extract structured brief. Read-only "brief card." Nothing removed.
P1
The compass. AI brief→treatment/script scaffold + the on-brief alignment scorecard (mandatories / key message / durations coverage, green/amber) projecting the brief forward into the live script & deliverables.
P2
TVC workflow profile + Deliverables module. Picking TVC seeds brief/treatment/deliverables, relabels nav, collapses the stripboard to a shot list; build the Deliverables & Versions cutdown×ratio matrix with the post pipeline + delivery deadlines into schedule/cost.
P3
Commercial controls. Usage/buyout + holding-fee clock + music licences with expiry alerts; PPM sign-off gate; optional AICP bid view of the budget; agency/brand stakeholders.

7 Decisions for you

A · Where does the brief live?
Extend the existing Script hub into a "Creative hub" (Brief · Treatment · Script · Boards · Deliverables) (recommended — it's already the creative spine), vs a separate Brief module.
B · How far should the AI go from the brief?
AI scaffolds an editable treatment + script draft and runs the on-brief compass (recommended — fastest producer value), vs alignment/compass only (no generation), vs full auto-generate.
C · Project-type behaviour.
A workflow profile per type that flips nav/terminology/seeds/module-emphasis (recommended — scales to Corporate, Music Video, Doc too), vs hardcoded TVC-only branches.
D · Is the Deliverables matrix the TVC spine?
Yes — Deliverables & Versions becomes the primary TVC workspace (recommended), with scenes/strips demoted to a shot list; vs keep the stripboard primary and add deliverables as a side panel.
Net effect. "TVC" stops being a label and becomes a real mode: the client's brief is read once by AI and carried forward as a living compass, the producer always sees whether they're on-brief, and the project is organised around the deliverables the client actually bought — while every shared prep/shoot/wrap tool you already built keeps working underneath.
