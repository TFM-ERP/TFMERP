64 — Four hours invisible

A timezone skew that hides exactly the window an investigation asks about

Every createdAt in this schema is timestamp without time zone holding UTC. The database session runs TimeZone = Asia/Dubai. So now(), and any JavaScript Date handed to a raw query, arrives as +04 wall time, and the comparison misses by four hours in the one direction that costs something: it hides the most recent rows.

The same predicate, against the same table, at the same instant, disagrees with itself by two rows — and the two it drops are the two created in the last quarter of an hour.

What it cost, on the night it was found: asked whether a second build row existed, the first form returned zero, and the answer “there is no second build” was one sentence from being published. The second build existed, was nine minutes old, and was the entire subject of the question. A skew that hides only the newest rows is invisible to every query except the one that matters.

Scope was checked rather than assumed. No raw query in the service carries a date predicate, and Prisma’s typed queries convert consistently; production is not affected today. The rule is for anything hand-written against these columns — harness, migration, one-off, psql session — and the correct form is (now() AT TIME ZONE 'UTC') or an explicit UTC literal.

The first diagnosis of this was “Prisma does not bind Date parameters here” — arrived at by testing the consequence, that the query returned nothing, rather than the mechanism. It was wrong, and it would have sent the next reader into the driver. A defect explained by its symptom will be re-diagnosed by everyone who meets it again.

WHERE "createdAt" >= now() - interval '3 hours'                      -> 0 rows
WHERE "createdAt" >= (now() AT TIME ZONE 'UTC') - interval '3 hours' -> 2 rows

65 — The guarantee sits below the loss

Undroppability held perfectly and bought nothing

Two canon extractions of the same bible, four days apart, same extractor version 2, same CANON_FACT_CAP = 120, sources differing by a single character. PROHIBITION came back 90 the first time and 80 the second.

The quota discarded nothing in either run — kept 208 / dropped 0 and kept 210 / dropped 0. So §36’s protection did exactly what it was built to do, and it changed nothing: PROHIBITION was made undroppable so the quota could not discard it, and the ten that went missing were never offered to the quota in the first place. A guarantee placed above the stage where the loss occurs is a guarantee about the wrong stage.

The stored account cannot tell the two cases apart. kept 210, dropped 0, shortfall "" reads identically whether eighty prohibitions exist in the material or ninety exist and ten were missed. Nothing recorded distinguishes extracted everything there was from extracted everything it happened to notice, so the count is evidence of what one pass produced and not of coverage.

This is not the ±2 of §34’s 23/20/22. It is eleven per cent of the largest category, from a one-character source edit, in the category the register treats as undroppable.

66 — One character, four and a half minutes

The price of a correct key, and the comparison it invalidates

sourceCanonFor keys stored canon on a sha256 of the trimmed source. Two bibles differing by one character hash differently, find no stored row, and extract from scratch. On 14 Sep that cost four and a half minutes and one 107,763-character call, while the build whose text matched to the byte reused a four-day-old row in milliseconds and made no model call at all.

This is correct behaviour and should not be filed as a bug. It is the price of replacing §34’s 360-character key, which collided across genuinely different sources. A whole-source digest cannot produce a false hit; what it cannot do is notice that two sources are nearly the same. Anyone who edits a bible should expect the next build to pay for a fresh extraction.

The consequence that is easy to miss: the two rows in §65 look like a stability measurement of the same input and are not one. Any comparison of fact counts across builds must check the digest before it means anything.

67 — Nothing attempted, nothing contradicted

A clean register score measures what the draft reached for, not how well it reached

Two builds, one bible, the same register. Both canon rows carry 81 register lines and the sets are identical — nothing only in one, nothing only in the other — so the check put the same 81 statements to both drafts. The only structural difference between the builds is the direction row.

cmu1o0xeh has none. Its prompts carried no KEEP list, its drafts reached for none of the named material, and it scored 0 / 0 / 0 across LOGLINE, SYNOPSIS and TREATMENT. cmu1ocawn has the FAITHFUL row, reached for the lines that row names, and scored 1 / 2 / 1. Two of those four contradictions are on KEEP-list lines the direction named — the same lines the keep check independently reports as misses. The build that tried to honour the material is the build the register caught.

A draft that attempts none of the fixed material cannot contradict it, so a low contradiction rate measures reach before it measures fidelity. A zero is produced by fidelity and by avoidance alike, and the score alone does not say which.

Scope. This holds where the controlled pair exists — LOGLINE, SYNOPSIS and TREATMENT — and is UNTESTED at SCENES. The SCENES numbers available are a third build (§54's, on the §58 error-message brief, direction state unknown) and a run of cmu1ocawn made with no BEATS stage on the build at all: two uncontrolled variables, and both SCENES scores happen to read 1 of 81. The finding stands on the pair and claims nothing beyond it. The discipline §67 applies to §54 applies to §67.

The consequence reaches backwards. §54 records “1 of 81 contradicted” as the first quality measurement ever taken on SCENES, and that number licensed the bounded-thinking result. It was taken on a build with no direction row. The measurement stands exactly as taken; what it now needs is its reach denominator — how much of the register that draft was trying to honour at all — before the rate can be read as fidelity.
