The thesis in one line

Craft tools help you write. Coverage tools tell you what's there. Prediction tools tell you what it's worth. Pre-production tools turn a script into a budget. Nobody closes the loop — constraint in → rewritten script out → re-costed → re-covered. That loop is only possible inside a system that already owns all the data. That system is TFM. And it is global by design — a worldwide library of rating boards and markets, with the Middle East as a first-class home rather than an afterthought.

Cinelytic will tell a studio that swapping the lead or shifting the genre raises projected revenue. It will not write the new script. Greenlight will tell you the midpoint sags. It will not fix it or show the budget impact of the fix. The white space is the verb: re-create, grounded in real production data.

The market, in three silos that never connect

Craft / generative

Sudowrite (Story Engine, Sensory Rewrite), Storyflow, Final Draft Beat Board, FinalBit, Arc Studio, Squibler/Melies

Help you WRITE — beats, prose, dialogue, story bibles, novel→script, series bibles.

Where it stops: Stops at the page. No production reality, no budget, no coverage verdict, no market signal.

Analysis / coverage

ScriptReader.ai, Greenlight Coverage, Quanten Arc, Story Arc Analyzer, Koobrik

Tell you what's THERE — coverage prose, Pass/Consider/Recommend, arc graphs, stats (dialogue vs action %, conflict).

Where it stops: Good at stats, 'falls flat at troubleshooting problems and creating more engaging scenes' (users' words). Diagnoses, doesn't fix or re-cost.

Prediction / market

Largo.ai, Cinelytic

Tell you what it's WORTH — genre/dramaturgy/character analysis, casting propositions, box-office forecast; Cinelytic 'what-if' on lead actor / genre blend → projected revenue.

Where it stops: What-if is financial only. It never returns the rewritten script that the what-if implies.

Pre-production AI

Filmustage, Studiovity

Turn script INTO logistics — any-language breakdown in minutes, schedule, budget categories, conflict surfacing.

Where it stops: One-way: script → budget. Never budget → revised script.

One tool, three lenses

Producer mode

Make this real / cheaper / safer.

Asks the production questions (below); returns budget/location-fit rewrites, culture & compliance flags, coverage with Production feasibility, and the cost/schedule delta of every option.

Writer mode

Make this better.

Scene diagnostics (who-wants-what, obstacle, subtext, power-shift), structure & arc analysis, dialogue punch-up, before/after rewrites, character-arc and emotion mapping — agency stays with the writer.

Market mode

Make this sell.

Audience-fit, comps, genre-blend and tone options, format/market potential, hook/cliffhanger strength, plus the Market & Greenlight forecast — and the prescription to lift it.

The constraint intake — the production questions

Producer mode opens by asking what the production can actually carry. Every downstream rewrite, option and coverage note is bound to these answers.

  • Budget ceiling (and target % saving: 10 / 20 / 50%)
  • Shoot-day count
  • Max distinct locations / sets
  • Cast size (speaking roles)
  • Filming country & culture/rating constraints
  • Target genre / genre-blend
  • Target audience (age, gender, region)
  • Format (feature / series / vertical micro-drama)
  • Comparable titles to aim at
  • Hard 'must-keep' scenes, characters or beats

The re-creation engine

Each transform returns a slate of labelled variants with tradeoffs — never a single take — and each variant branches as a real, costable Script Hub revision.

Budget / location fit
"Bring it to $X / N days / M locations."
Multi-variant output, e.g.: Consolidate 11 locations → 6 by merging the diner + bar + lobby into one hub; move the night chase to dusk; replace the helicopter insert with a sound-design beat. New estimate + strip impact shown.
Protagonist swap / POV recentre
"Make it about the sidekick (or the antagonist)."
Multi-variant output, e.g.: V1: keep plot, retell through Leo's eyes (he becomes the moral centre). V2: promote Leo to lead and demote Mark to mentor. V3: dual-POV braid. Each re-checks arc & structure.
Emotion re-key
"Less bleak — end on earned hope," or "colder, more dread."
Multi-variant output, e.g.: Re-scores the emotional arc beat-by-beat; rewrites 4 pivot scenes to shift the curve; shows the before/after emotion graph.
Genre transpose
"Same story as a thriller / as a dark comedy."
Multi-variant output, e.g.: Re-tone dialogue and set-pieces to genre conventions; flag which scenes carry the new genre; keep the spine. Returns 2–3 tonal takes.
Ending re-engineering
"Give me other endings — not just an open one."
Multi-variant output, e.g.: Offers the full taxonomy as labelled options — resolved/happy, tragic, bittersweet, twist, ambiguous/open, circular, ironic, epilogue/flash-forward, hopeful-redemptive, cliffhanger — each with the earlier set-ups it needs and the feeling it leaves.
Add / remove a character
"Cut the partner; fold their function into the rival."
Multi-variant output, e.g.: Re-assigns the cut character's scenes/lines/function, re-checks who-wants-what in affected scenes, updates cast-days and coverage character breakdown.
Format conversion
"Feature → 8-ep series, → short film, or → vertical micro-drama," or "story → full script."
Multi-variant output, e.g.: Re-beats into TV episodes/acts, a tight short, or SEERA-grade Arabic vertical units (60–90s: scenic objective → power/info shift → escalation → hook; 40–80-ep seasons) with the ‘yes-but / no-also’ addiction loop; generates a series bible; or expands a logline into a full draft.
Structural surgery
"Fix the saggy midpoint / weak Act 2."
Multi-variant output, e.g.: Diagnoses against Save the Cat / Hero's Journey / sequence / 5-act TV; repositions the inciting incident or midpoint; trims to a leaner page count.
Rating & market fit
"Bring this R down to a PG-13," or "a GCC-compliant cut."
Multi-variant output, e.g.: V1: minimal-edit PG-13 — soften 3 dialogue lines, move one act off-screen, cut one gore detail. V2: BBFC 15 cut. V3: GCC-compliant cut — language + 2 visual beats + one entity swap. Each a branchable revision with a content-descriptor diff.
Country / location transposition
"Re-set it to shoot in the UAE / Saudi / Egypt," or "localise for the GCC market."
Multi-variant output, e.g.: V1: relocate to Abu Dhabi — swap 3 locations for available equivalents and hit the cultural-test for the rebate. V2: a KSA setting with culturally-native family dynamics. V3: a market-localised GCC OTT cut. Each shows incentive + cost impact.
Adaptation: book / PDF → script
"Here's a novel — what films are in it?"
Multi-variant output, e.g.: V1: a biopic focused on the 3 pivotal years. V2: a romance built on the A–B thread. V3: a thriller pulled from the Z subplot. Each names the focal character, the composite-character plan and a comp — then expands the one you pick.
Version compare & change report
"What changed between the blue and white revision?"
Multi-variant output, e.g.: Scene-by-scene diff (added / cut / modified), dialogue and character-presence changes, page-count delta, and the budget & schedule impact of the changes — a clean, producer-ready report.
Humour injection (by culture)
"Add Egyptian wordplay," "dry British wit," or "warm GCC family comedy."
Multi-variant output, e.g.: Name the culture/country and dial light→dark; adds callbacks, running gags, irony, banter or satire in that tradition (British understatement vs American observational vs manzai timing) without breaking character voice — several takes to choose from.
Scene intensity & tone dial
Select one scene, several, or the whole script: "more intense," "more suspense," "retone to horror / heroic / romance."
Multi-variant output, e.g.: Dials intensity up or down and re-tones toward a target genre/emotion, regenerating as many labelled variants as you want — at scene, sequence or whole-script scope.
Develop: seed → script (any format)
"Here's a paragraph / treatment / bible — build it out."
Multi-variant output, e.g.: Researches comparables (and the real person for a biopic), interviews you on genre, format, protagonist, theme and ending, then climbs logline→synopsis→treatment→beats→scenes→draft as a feature, series, short or vertical micro-drama.
Cost-optimization scan (new project)
"Where can this project save 20%?"
Multi-variant output, e.g.: Ranks quantified savings — consolidate these 4 locations, cluster the lead's shoot days, take the X-country rebate, convert 3 nights to day — each with the dollar impact and a one-click apply.
Audience-fit pass
"Optimise for women 18–34," or "widen to four-quadrant."
Multi-variant output, e.g.: Scores resonance, representation and quadrant reach, checks the psychological truth, and proposes targeted changes to sharpen or broaden the audience.

Ingestion & formats — bring a script in from anywhere

A story should not be trapped in the app it was written in. The engine ingests the formats the industry actually uses, and degrades gracefully when a file is messy.

Formats it reads

  • Final Draft (.fdx, XML)
  • Fountain (.fountain / .txt)
  • Celtx
  • Microsoft Word (.docx)
  • Apple Pages
  • RTF · HTML · plain text
  • PDF — text and scanned

When the file is messy

  • OCR for scanned / image PDFs — Image-only or photographed pages run through OCR (Tesseract) so even a snapshot of a script becomes editable text.
  • Tolerant by design — OCR and parsing are never perfect — low-confidence or garbled passages are flagged and the script imports anyway, fixable later. Import never blocks the work.
  • One model downstream — However it arrives, it lands in the same scene model the whole engine uses — breakdown, schedule, budget, coverage and every transform.

Adaptation studio — any story becomes a script

Drop in a novel, a manuscript or a PDF. The engine reads it the way a development exec does — finds the films hiding inside it, shows you the directions you could take, and then takes its time crafting the one you choose toward award level.

How it reads a source

  • Theme & spine first — Identify the theme and the protagonist's outer motivation; whatever doesn't serve them is a candidate to cut.
  • Character map + composites — Map every character, then merge two or three book characters into one screen character.
  • Extractable threads — Surface the storylines hiding in the source — each is a potential film.
  • Focus-narrowing — Compress to the events that resonate, the way Jackie lives in a few days or The Imitation Game in the Enigma years.

Directions it can derive

  • Biopic — focus the pivotal years / one defining event
  • Romance — build on a central relationship thread
  • Drama — the moral or internal conflict at the core
  • Thriller / crime — pull a tense subplot to the front
  • Coming-of-age — the early chapters as the whole film
  • Comedy or sci-fi re-framing where the premise allows

Then it goes deep (multi-pass)

  • Direction & focal thread chosen with you
  • Outline -> beats (Save the Cat / Hero's Journey / sequence)
  • Scenes built with the doctor's diagnostics (objective, obstacle, subtext, power-shift)
  • Dialogue polish for distinct voices
  • A coverage pass to check it lands — iterate until it does

Learns from how the best adaptations actually work

  • The Imitation Game — a whole life narrowed to the Enigma operation
  • A Beautiful Mind — a maths biography re-keyed as romance + drama
  • The Social Network — a nonfiction book pulled into a legal drama
  • Lion — a three-decade memoir focused into one search
  • Jackie — compressed to a few claustrophobic days
The source is read into TFM's scene model, so the chosen draft flows straight on to breakdown, schedule, budget, rating and coverage — the adaptation and the production live in one place. And because it returns a slate of directions first, you pick the film before a single scene is written.

Development studio — from a seed to a finished script

Start from anything — a paragraph, a logline, a treatment, a whole series bible, even a real person — and the engine researches, interviews you, and climbs the industry development ladder to a finished script in the format you choose.

Start from anything

  • A paragraph or a premise
  • A logline (25–35 words)
  • A 1–3 page synopsis
  • A 5–20 page treatment
  • A series bible
  • Character notes
  • A real person or event (biopic)

It researches before it writes

  • Comparable works — Pulls similar treatments and films to position the piece and find the fresh angle.
  • The real subject — For a biopic, researches the actual person and events — what to honour, what to dramatise.
  • Market read — Shares the Greenlight engine's comps and audience signal, so development starts market-aware.

Then it interviews you

  • Genre & tone
  • Target format — feature / TV series / short / vertical micro-drama — and length
  • Protagonist + want vs need
  • The opposing force
  • Theme
  • World & setting
  • Target audience
  • Ending type (from the taxonomy)
  • Comps to aim at
  • Hard constraints (budget, country, rating)

Then it climbs the craft ladder

  • Logline → synopsis → treatment
  • Beats / outline (Field · Snyder · McKee · Truby · Yorke · sequence)
  • Scenes built with the doctor's diagnostics
  • Draft, then a coverage pass — iterate toward award level

Target-audience perspective

Every read can be taken from the audience’s seat, not just the writer’s. Define who the film is for, then judge and tune the script from their perspective.

Define the audience

  • Demographic quadrants — male/female × over/under 25 (four-quadrant reach)
  • Psychographics — values, interests, attitudes, lifestyles
  • Region & cultural context (ties to the culture screen)

Score the fit, then act

  • Resonance with the target audience
  • Representation & authenticity
  • Psychological / psychiatric truth of character behaviour
  • Quadrant reach (budget tracks the number of quadrants reached)
  • What this audience actually wants from the genre

Feeds coverage (target audience + marketability), the market forecast and the rewrite engine — recommend changes to widen toward four-quadrant or sharpen for one segment.

Green-lighting process (GLP) — the capstone

A real, repeatable green-lighting process — the committee stage-gates studios actually use — run on your project, synthesising everything above into a go / no-go you can defend.

The gates

  • 1 · Concept & coverage — Data-grounded coverage score + logline strength.
  • 2 · Package — Script + director + cast bankability and fit.
  • 3 · Audience & market — Four-quadrant / psychographic fit + the Greenlight forecast.
  • 4 · Budget, finance & incentives — Greenlight model — revenue across windows vs negative cost; base vs high case; incentive plan.
  • 5 · Risk & IP — Risk register; franchise / spin-off / merch potential.
  • 6 · Strategy & brand fit — Does it fit the slate and the company’s brand?
  • 7 · Decision — Weighted scorecard + one-page decision memo + committee sign-off; re-check assumptions before production.

What it produces

  • A weighted green-light scorecard
  • A base-case vs high-case financial model
  • A risk register
  • A one-page decision memo for the committee

Why only TFM

The script score, the real budget, the schedule, the audience read and the market forecast already live in one place — so the green-light model is built from facts, not estimates, and the moment it is a “go”, the same data carries straight into production.

  • The Production section is computed from the breakdown — real location count, cast-days, night shoots, VFX load — then explained in prose.
  • Comps & budget tier draw on structured genre/scale data, not a vibe.
  • Coverage re-runs automatically after a rewrite, so the score always matches the current draft.

Scene diagnostics

  • Who wants what? (every character has an active objective)
  • What is the obstacle? (no resistance = no drama)
  • What is the subtext? (the real conflict is between the lines)
  • How does the power dynamic shift? (no shift = cut the scene)

Ending taxonomy

  • Resolved / happy — hero gets want + need
  • Tragic — loss or defeat for impact
  • Bittersweet — wins the want, not the need
  • Twist — hidden info flips the meaning
  • Ambiguous / open — the audience finishes it
  • Circular — echoes the opening
  • Ironic — the opposite of what was sought
  • Epilogue / flash-forward — life after the end
  • Hopeful / redemptive — earned light
  • Cliffhanger — unresolved, pulls to the next

Rating & market-fit engine

A detailed, tooled solution for one of the most variable problems in the industry: making the same story land a different certificate and play in a different market — across wording, visuals and entities — without breaking the film. Cinelytic predicts how a market reacts; Spherex tags finished video for distribution. Neither re-writes the script to hit a target rating. TFM can.

Target systems

  • A GLOBAL library — every major board worldwide; examples below
  • MPA (US): G·PG·PG-13·R·NC-17 · BBFC (UK): U·PG·12A·15·18
  • FSK (Germany) · CNC (France) · CBFC India (U/UA/A/S) · Eirin (Japan) · KMRB (Korea) · ACB (Australia) · ClassInd (Brazil) · NRTA (China content review)
  • UAE (National Media Authority): G·PG·PG13·PG15·15+·18+·21+
  • KSA (GCAM/GAMR): G·PG12·PG15·R15·R18 · other GCC: Qatar, Egypt, Kuwait, Bahrain
  • Streaming maturity TV-Y…TV-MA/18+ · per-market censorship norms (theatrical vs OTT)

The variables it scores

  • Language & profanity — count + severity (one non-sexual F-word is the PG-13 line)
  • Violence & gore — intensity, blood, weapons, peril, realism
  • Sex & nudity — explicitness and duration
  • Substance use — drugs/alcohol/smoking, especially by minors
  • Frightening / intense scenes — horror intensity, threat
  • Mature themes — suicide, abuse, self-harm and how depicted
  • Hate / discriminatory language & slurs
  • Religious & cultural sensitivities (ties to the culture screen)

What it outputs

  • A content-descriptor profile (severity per dimension) + an estimated certificate for each board
  • A target-rating diff: the exact lines/scenes pushing it over, and the minimal-edit set to land the target
  • A per-market variant slate — US PG-13 theatrical, UK 15, GCC-compliant, unrated streaming — each a branchable revision
  • A "why this rating" rationale citing the triggering content, ready for human QC

The levers it pulls

Every adjustment is a labelled, reversible edit — the producer sees the minimal set of changes that moves the certificate, and can accept them per layer.

LayerHow it adjusts content to hit the target
WordingSoften or trim dialogue, replace explicit references with implication, remove slurs.
Visual / action linesMove violence or sex off-screen, reduce gore detail, change a depicted act to an implied one.
StructuralTrim, shorten or relocate an intense beat; add context that re-frames it.
Entity / propSwap a real brand, flag, symbol or substance for a compliant equivalent.
Because TFM binds the content-descriptor profile to the breakdown and the distribution plan, Market mode can show the cost of the rating itself — e.g. "staying R closes these territories and ~X% of the audience; here is the PG-13 cut that re-opens them, and the 6 edits it needs."

Architecture — built right, not bolted on

The release-readiness audit flagged TFM's AI as fragmented — ten services each calling the model directly. Script Doctor is the chance to fix that by being the first citizen of a proper AI layer, not the tenth offender.

  • One AI service, not a 10th call site — Script Doctor is the flagship app of the centralised AI layer the release audit recommended — a single AiService (model config, retries, streaming, token accounting) instead of another direct Anthropic call.
  • Governance spine — Every output carries prompt+model version, a confidence score, source citations (for culture/comps), a variant lineage, a PENDING→APPROVED lifecycle and a central AiRun audit record.
  • Reuse the SSOT — Reads the Script Hub scene model, tag-once breakdown, projection→strips, budget EFC and casting/locations — never re-implements them. Coverage numbers come from this data.
  • Variants = real revisions — Each option branches as a colour-coded Script Hub revision the producer can diff, cost and adopt or discard. Nothing auto-commits.
  • Closed loop wiring — A rewrite triggers a re-projection to strips → re-cost in the budget engine → regenerated coverage score, all shown as a single before/after delta.

Governance & trust

  • AI proposes, human disposes — every creative change is a suggestion the writer/producer accepts, edits or rejects.
  • Culture/compliance flags cite the rule and recommend a human local advisor; the tool screens and suggests, it does not certify (matches industry practice).
  • Provenance on every generated line so a later WGA credit/authorship question is answerable.
  • Confidence + 'needs human review' banding; low-confidence fixes are flagged, never silently applied.
  • Fix-tracking workflow: each identified problem is a ticket — open → addressed → verified — so a rewrite pass is auditable.
  • Ratings are decided by certification boards, not the tool: the engine ESTIMATES a certificate, lists the exact triggers, prepares the cut, and flags it for human QC — it never guarantees a rating.
